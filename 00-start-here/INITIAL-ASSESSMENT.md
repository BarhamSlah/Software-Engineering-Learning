# Initial Software Engineering Assessment

## Purpose

This assessment identifies what I already understand. It is not an exam and it is not a score of my ability as a developer. Its purpose is to choose the right starting point.

Answer in your own words. Short answers are fine. If you do not know an answer, say so. Do not search for answers while taking the assessment.

## Part A — Core understanding

1. What is the difference between **programming** and **software engineering**?
2. Imagine a customer asks a company to build a banking application. What major steps do you think the team should follow before and after releasing it?
3. What is the difference between a **functional requirement** and a **non-functional requirement**? Give one example of each.
4. Why is testing important even when an application appears to work correctly?
5. What does **maintainable software** mean?

## Part B — Working with a team

6. A developer changes a feature and wants another developer to review it before it becomes part of the main codebase. What Git/GitHub workflow could support this, and why?
7. What is the purpose of a code review?
8. Who might be involved in delivering a software feature besides the programmer? What might each person contribute?

## Part C — Backend thinking

9. A Java application needs to save customer accounts and balances after the application closes. What kind of system would you use to store this information, and why?
10. In a backend application, what is the difference between an API and a database?
11. A user sends a request to transfer money, but the receiving account does not exist. What should a reliable application consider before reporting success?
12. What do you understand by deployment?

## Part D — Confidence check

13. Which topics above can you explain confidently?
14. Which topics have you heard about but do not fully understand?
15. What have you already studied or used in your own Java projects?

## Assessment method

The mentor should review the answers and identify:

- **Strong knowledge:** correct explanation and appropriate example.
- **Partial knowledge:** main idea is understood, but important details are missing.
- **Needs learning:** explanation is incorrect, unclear, or missing.
- **Not assessed:** no answer or insufficient evidence.

Then create a personalized starting sequence. Do not force the learner to repeat topics they already understand. Do not skip important gaps just because the learner recognizes the terminology.
